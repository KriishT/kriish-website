## The problem

Thoughts arrive at bad times: halfway through a function, a paragraph, a call. Stopping to file one breaks the work; not filing it loses it. Tangent is a local-first desktop app for Windows and macOS built around that moment.

## My role

I built Tangent on my own. It took about a month to build, test and keep improving. A good part of that went into the Mac build: Apple flags apps that aren’t from a verified developer, and I haven’t paid for the $99-a-year Apple developer account, so I had to work out how to get people past those security warnings.

## The hard interaction: capture without leaving

- A global shortcut opens quick capture over whatever you’re doing. Type, or hold to speak; speech is transcribed on the device with Whisper.
- Each note saves the app and the window or file you were in, automatically.

You press the shortcut and it’s done. There’s no switching tabs and no reaching for your phone, and the thought is stored in the app. Notifications bring it back later, when you can deal with it.

## Decisions and tradeoffs

### Local-first, no account

Notes live in a local SQLite database, and there is no sign-up. Installers ship through GitHub Releases.

<Figure src={privacy} alt="Tangent’s privacy section: no account, no telemetry; your data in a plain SQLite file; optional on-device voice with Whisper; work-context capture you can switch off or limit per app" caption="Local-first, as Tangent’s site promises it: no account, no telemetry, and a plain SQLite file you own." />

I didn’t give up anything I needed. Tangent is version 1 and I’m not planning to charge for it, so keeping everything on the device is the simplest way to keep it safe and earn people’s trust.

### Triage by keyboard

Notes move through five buckets (Do Now, Do Soon, Later, Idea and Drop) without touching the mouse.

At first I merged Do Now and Do Soon into a single bucket. Users told me the difference mattered, so I split them in two.

### Dates in plain words

Due dates are typed the way people say them, “tomorrow” or “Friday 3pm”. Follow-ups can go to Google Calendar or to native OS reminders.

## Outcome

- Installers for Windows and macOS are public on GitHub Releases.
- GitHub’s raw download count across all releases is 150, as of October 2026. That includes the files the app fetches to check for updates; the installers themselves account for 51 of them.
- People have given good feedback on how easy Tangent is to use and how easy the Windows download is. The Mac setup still needs to be easier.

## What I’d do next

- **An anchor on screen.** A small element that stays visible, so that seeing it reminds you to capture a thought.
- **Easier Mac setup.** Smoothing the path past Apple’s security warnings.
- **Making it more robust**, as more people use it.
